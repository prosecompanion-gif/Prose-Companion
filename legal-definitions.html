Word of the Day

When you're inside the courthouse — or writing and reading any papers that came from or are being submitted to the court — there are words that can trip you up.

These are words we all use in our everyday language without ever thinking about what they mean. They're so common and ordinary that we never need to. But one of the many things you should know — and are never informed of — is that inside the courthouse, these same words have very different meanings.

They even have their very own dictionary.

Unfortunately for those of us on a budget, the current version — with over 2,500 newly added words to keep up with technology and the times — isn't available anywhere for free. You can access the version from six editions ago, but that doesn't help you now. There is no web version of their official dictionary. If you have $150 a month to pay for a legal resource website membership, you can view it there. Or your option is to buy the dictionary, available only in hardcover, for $200.

Most people can't afford that.

A friend purchased one for me for my birthday, and I'm going to use it. Each week, I'll pick 2 or 3 words from the comments on each episode's YouTube page, and the following week at the end of the episode, I'll read off the definitions and add them to this page.

Here are a few examples to get us started:

Capacity

Everyday

How much someone or something can do or hold; size or volume.

Legal

The legal ability or status to sue, be sued, contract, inherit, or act for another.

Prayer

Everyday

An address to God or a god, spoken or thought; an earnest request; the act of praying; a religious service made up chiefly of prayers.

Legal

A request for relief in a legal complaint. A special prayer asks for particular relief, while a general prayer asks for additional, unspecified relief.

Conversion

Everyday

A change from one form, use, or condition to another.

Legal

A civil wrong involving wrongful possession, control, or dominion over another person's personal property.

Tender

Everyday

Gentle or soft; can also refer to an offer.

Legal

An unconditional offer to pay money or to perform what is owed.

Instrument

Everyday

A tool, device, or implement; also a musical device used to produce sound.

Legal

A formal legal document, such as a deed, will, promissory note, contract, or bond, that creates, records, or transfers rights or obligations.

Sit

Everyday

Resting in a seated position; staying in one place; being located somewhere.

Legal

Of a judge, to occupy a judicial seat, hold court, or perform official functions. Of a court or legislative body, to hold proceedings.

Sharp

Everyday

Having an edge; quick-witted; clearly defined.

Legal

A clause in a mortgage deed or similar instrument that allows the creditor to take immediate and summary action upon the debtor's default.

Sheer

Everyday

Very thin and see-through; complete or total; very steep.

Legal

In maritime law, a vessel's sudden deviation from its line of course — a swerve.

Shelving

Everyday

The shelves themselves; material used to make shelves; sometimes a sloping surface.

Legal

In patent law, failing to begin commercial use, or stopping commercial use, of a patented product during the relevant period.

Serve

Everyday

Helping, providing for, or performing a duty for someone; serving food; putting the ball in play in sports.

Legal

To make legal delivery of a notice or legal process, or to present a person with that notice or process as required by law.

Sentence

Everyday

A group of words that expresses a complete thought, usually with a subject and a verb.

Legal

In criminal procedure, the judgment a court formally pronounces after a finding of guilt, including the punishment imposed on the offender.

Relief

Everyday

The easing of pain, stress, or trouble; help given to people in need.

Legal

An heir's payment to a feudal lord for the privilege of succeeding to an ancestor's tenancy; also aid or assistance provided by the state.
